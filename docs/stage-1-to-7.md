# The stages explained

Seven stages. The complaint that shaped the second half of this project was that
they felt like one run with a different backdrop, and that was fair. They no
longer are. Each one asks something the others do not, and this is what each one
is for.

Everything here is generated from the day's market. The numbers below are the
fixed part: the clock, how crowded it gets, what you have to do to pass. The
level itself is different every morning.

| | Stage | World | Clock | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily Humiliation Ritual | Chart | 1:50 | Fly it |
| 2 | Shutdown Tour | Chart | 1:50 | Carry things out |
| 3 | Exploit Afterparty | Chart | 1:45 | Fight |
| 4 | Clarity Circus | Chart, watched | 1:40 | Do not be seen |
| 5 | Institutional Face Escort | City | 1:40 | Drive, or walk |
| 6 | Narrative Thunderdome | City | 2:30 | Read |
| 7 | Save Face | Ring city | 4:10 | Work it out |

---

## 1. Daily Humiliation Ritual

**Restores nerve, on the worst chart of the day.**
**To pass:** survive, and take the relic.

The introduction, and the only stage that is just the game. Today's worst
performer in the top 100 becomes the terrain: its real 24 hour chart, pulled at
midnight UTC, is the ground you fly over. A violent morning is a wall you have
to climb. A slow bleed is a long drop with nowhere to stand.

Five people are trapped in it. They are whoever crypto X actually spent the day
arguing about, with their real handles and pictures, and every line the game
shows carries a link back to the post it came from.

Twelve attackers, the emptiest sky in the game. Nothing here is hidden from you
and nothing is watching. Fly, free people, reach the pad.

## 2. Shutdown Tour

**Restores everything that went dark without a note.**
**To pass:** four caches out, and two people to the pad.

Same sky, more of it. The stage turns from "can you fly this" into "can you
carry things while flying this", which are different questions. Somebody on your
back changes how the ship handles, and one of the five is heavy enough that
carrying him is a real cost rather than a label.

Twenty seven attackers now. The clock is the same and there is more to do in it.

## 3. Exploit Afterparty

**Restores trust, after somebody found the hole.**
**To pass:** clear twelve attackers, and finish above half hull.

The first stage you cannot avoid your way through. It is the only clear
condition in the campaign that asks for a hull number, so getting the kills is
not enough on its own: you have to get them without being taken apart doing it.

Thirty two attackers, five seconds less on the clock, and the first stage where
your choice of gun is a decision rather than a preference.

## 4. Clarity Circus

**Restores the rules, such as they are.**
**To pass:** the relic, six caches, and reach the pad.

The last chart stage, and the one that changes what an attacker is. From here on
they have **sight cones**. Cross one and the level wakes: they stop patrolling
and start walking toward the noise, and the ones that have not seen you can be
walked around entirely.

That turns a fight into a decision about whether to have one. Thirty five
attackers on the board, and the fastest way through is to not be noticed by most
of them.

## 5. Institutional Face Escort

**Restores the case that any of this is real.**
**To pass:** get four people out alive.

The chart is gone. The same price data, drawn as **bars** instead of a line, is
already a skyline, so the streets between the buildings are the gaps between the
bars. Same seed, same numbers, projected differently. A violent day gives a
jagged city full of cover. A flat day gives long open avenues with nowhere to
hide.

There is a car. It is twice as fast and heard from twice as far, and a doorway
fits a person but not the car, so going inside means getting out. Driving
through somebody at speed hurts them, and twice puts them down.

Forty attackers, and the clear condition is the strictest in the first six:
everybody comes out or nobody does.

## 6. Narrative Thunderdome

**Restores the story, off whoever is shouting loudest.**
**To pass:** read every panel, then get out.

The only stage where the verb is reading. Panels around the district each show
**four posts that genuinely went out today**. One of them explains why the day
happened. Pick it and the panel flips. Pick wrong and everyone within a street
starts walking toward the noise.

The exit does not open until every panel is read, so this is not optional
content with a bonus attached. It is the stage.

Two and a half minutes, which is the longest clock before the finale and
deliberately so. Ten panels of four posts each at two minutes was twelve seconds
a panel including getting there, which is skimming for the shape of an answer
rather than reading one. A timer that punishes reading turns this stage into
guessing, which is the one thing it exists not to be.

Forty five attackers, the most in the game.

## 7. Save Face

**Restores the season itself.**
**To pass:** everybody out, the relic, and eight caches.

A third geometry, and the only stage that is not about shooting.

**Concentric rings around a core.** You start outside and work in. There is no
forward, only closer and further out, and the only way through a wall is the one
gap in it. The gaps are deliberately offset so that leaving one never points at
the next, which is what makes this circling a wall rather than running a
corridor.

**No weapon opens a gate.** Each gap is guarded by a question about the projects
that were here before this cycle and will be here after it. Reaching one of them
hands you its intel: where it sits by size, and how it is holding up while today
falls apart. The gate then asks about projects behind it and shows only their
tickers, so the answer is knowable only if you went and looked. You can reach a
gate without having done that. You simply cannot answer it.

The allies are the largest projects by market capitalisation on the day you
play, off the same market call that picks the day's wreck. Not a curated list of
what anyone thinks deserves to be there. Stablecoins are filtered out on the
data, because a peg is engineered not to move and so has not survived anything.

Twenty two attackers, a third of what stage six fields. The finale is decided by
working out what is going on, and a crowded arena there would not make that
harder, it would drown it.

Four minutes and ten seconds, the longest run in the game by a clear minute.

---

## What carries between them

**Face** is the score, and it compounds. It opens later stages, weapons, and a
steadier gun.

**The relic** is a single object on most stages and three clear conditions
depend on it. It is always worth the detour.

**Caches** pay in the day's own token, which is the money inside a run. It dies
with the run and the ticker changes tomorrow, so nobody accumulates an advantage
and none of it is for sale.

**Aim assist** is free at a baseline for everyone and rises with progress. A
staked challenge pins both sides to the baseline, because two people betting on
one seed have to be playing the same game.
